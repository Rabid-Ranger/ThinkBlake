# Writing CTAs that belong in the video

The editorial rule is direct, natural phrasing: no conditional “If you…” openers, generic engagement bundles, invented scarcity or unsupported promises. This is Blake’s writing preference, not a claim that one word universally changes conversion rates.

## What the research supports

YouTube recommends making end screens and CTAs part of the overall experience and featuring content relevant to the video:
- https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/hiding-end-screens-what-youtube-creators-need-to-know/
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789?hl=en

Wistia recommends a clear, concise offer that matches the video and gives viewers useful value. Its observations concern hosted interactive CTAs/forms, not a controlled test of spoken organic YouTube scripts. We do not transfer its conversion percentages or treat one-third timing as universally optimal:
- https://wistia.com/blog/using-video-ctas
- https://wistia.com/blog/generating-leads-with-video

## Working method

Write the content bridge first: which demonstration, difficulty or next decision has made the destination useful? Then choose the real destination and describe its concrete benefit. Make one actionable invitation. An in-video invitation returns directly to the lesson; an ending carries the viewer into the next useful action or unresolved question. Keep destinations, resource contents and service commitments truthful. Test actual click/lead quality rather than assuming a clever sentence guarantees conversion.

The app provides different starting structures for a resource, a business/service, and another video, with separate in-video and ending wording. These are scaffolds to rewrite in the creator’s voice, not final scripts or new offers.

## Illustrative examples (fictional resources/services)

Resource within a demonstration: “I keep those three costs on one page before buying anything. The blank version is linked below to work through your own list. Now, the habit that costs nothing is the one I’d start with.”

Business invitation: “Choosing plants is one decision; fitting them to a small, shady garden is another. That’s what we work through in a garden-planning session. The details are below. First, here’s how to spot the space you’re actually working with.”

Next video: “The flowers are chosen. Keeping that bed in colour through August takes a different decision: what replaces the early blooms. This next video works through that planting sequence.”

Use examples only when the resource, service and content actually exist. The dashboard deliberately does not manufacture those facts for a client.
